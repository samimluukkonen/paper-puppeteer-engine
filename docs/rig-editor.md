# Rig editor

[All guides](../README.md#guides)

Open `/rig-editor/` from the demo's **Open Rig Editor** link. The editor loads the sample character with its default skin and shows its canonical bind pose in right facing. Select bones by clicking joints or their parent lines, or use the hierarchy. Select slots by clicking their square markers or hierarchy entries. Hold Alt while clicking when a slot overlaps a joint.

Use the header's **Character** selector to load either the demo puppet or the artist's **Random paperdoll sketch**. The sketch also opens directly at `/rig-editor/?character=random-paperdoll-sketch`. Navigation to the Engine Demo and Animation Editor keeps the selected character. Switching characters uses the same unsaved-edit protection as leaving the editor.

The sketch reference faces left. Its bind pose mirrors the original artwork and joints to face right, putting left/back limbs on screen right and right/front limbs on screen left.

The sketch's starter rig uses all 14 transparent part exports at 30% scale and an invisible pelvis joint. Front artwork maps to `right*` bones and back artwork to `left*` bones. The assembled `TEST 001 - Rando paperdoll sketch.png` is the alignment reference and is not loaded as a puppet texture. Refine joints and sprite origins in the editor, download `rig.json`, and replace `public/characters/random-paperdoll-sketch/rig.json` to use those changes after a refresh in any view.

All demo controls work with the sketch, including mouse-hand IK, planted-foot IK, the two-handed katana, sword animation and upper-body layer, equipment toggles, debug views, and pose editing. Walk and combat motions are adapted from the demo puppet to the sketch's joint positions. Facing left swaps the actual arm and leg chains, their cutouts, and draw depths, so front limbs use `left*` IDs and back limbs use `right*` IDs. Foot planting uses the corresponding leg geometry. The right hand retains the primary katana grip and grip-hand equipment in both directions; the left hand retains the secondary grip. Optional armor, grip-hand, headwear, and slot-katana textures reuse the demo puppet's artwork.

The sample's left shoulder and hip are on screen right, and its right shoulder and hip are on screen left, matching Engine Demo and Animation Editor. Left limbs draw behind the body; right limbs draw in front. The left arm's visual slots use explicit global depths to keep the entire arm behind the legs as well as the torso while retaining torso parenting.

Choose **Joint** to drag a joint and change its local position; descendants follow their parent transforms. Choose **Sprite** to drag selected artwork independently of its skeleton joint. A selected slot's square marker moves the slot; in Sprite mode, dragging its artwork moves the attachment offset. The inspector edits local x/y, rotation in radians, scale, logical length, sprite offset and normalized origin. It also shows degrees for reference. With a hierarchy entry focused, Up/Down select the previous/next entry, Home/End select the first/last entry, Left selects its parent, and Right selects its first child. These commands change selection without editing the rig; the hierarchy keeps one Tab stop. Outside the hierarchy and typing controls, arrow keys nudge a selected joint by one unit; Shift uses ten. Comma and period rotate by 0.01 radians; Shift uses 0.1. Inputs and drags update the preview immediately. When a parent has an explicit logical length, child x/y edits set the joint's direction while that length controls its distance in both the preview and the exported runtime rig. A zero child offset uses the parent's positive y axis. Logical length changes reposition direct child joints along their current directions and do not scale images.

Bone depth edits change preview draw order; enter a lower value to move a piece back or a higher value to move it forward. Slot depth is a global numeric draw depth. **Dim unselected artwork** helps expose overlapping parts. **IK Preview** pins the current chain endpoints and runs the existing solver as the rig changes; its temporary pose does not enter the working rig. Visibility controls show artwork, bones, names, lengths, slots, origins, and IK chains. Left-button drag on the canvas background moves the whole character preview, including its pinned IK targets, without changing rig values or undo history. A background click clears the selection; a background drag keeps it. Mouse wheel zoom and middle mouse pan affect only the camera.

Production workflow:

1. Start the rig editor and load the sample character. Select bones or slots in the hierarchy or canvas.
2. Position joints, then use Sprite mode or inspector fields to adjust artwork offsets and origins. Set logical bone lengths, slot transforms, and render depth as needed.
3. Preview IK. Use Grid (Off, 1, 2, 5, 10) for drag and nudge translation, and Rotation Snap (Off, 1°, 5°, 15°) for keyboard rotation. When Grid is on, the canvas shows lines aligned with the selected joint, slot, or artwork's local edit coordinates. At low zoom it shows a readable subset of those snap lines. Numeric fields retain exact values. Clear a numeric field and leave it to remove its authored override; omitted length and slot depth display Auto. Removing a length override keeps the current child offsets. Snapping never changes untouched values.
4. Undo with Ctrl/Cmd+Z and redo with Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z. A canvas drag or one numeric field focus session makes one history entry. A new edit after undo clears redo. **Reset Selected** restores the selected bone or slot and its active artwork attachment to session-start values; **Reset Rig** restores the whole session-start rig. Both resets are undoable.
5. Click **Validate**. Export uses the complete versioned rig validator and blocks errors, showing their paths and messages. The session panel reports character, file, mode, selection, snapping, modified state, and validation state.
6. Click **Copy rig JSON**, **Download rig.json**, or **Log rig JSON**. Each generates readable, stable, version 1 JSON with canonical local transforms. Edited offsets, angles, and origins use up to six decimal places, preserving small nonzero values that would otherwise round to zero. Scales, lengths, depths, numeric frame IDs, and untouched numbers retain their full precision so export cannot collapse a transform or change draw order through rounding. Export marks the current working copy as the saved baseline, so Modified becomes No. Further edits become dirty; undoing back to the exported state becomes clean.
7. Click **Reload exported rig** to rebuild the puppet preview from the last export and verify its appearance. **Reload original rig** starts a new session from the shipped file. **Load rig JSON** accepts a local JSON file for the same character and validates it before rebuilding the preview. The imported rig must use textures already loaded for that character.

The exported file keeps the rig name and IDs. Skin files from `character.json` stay separate, and embedded skins retain their authored definitions on export. Preview merges both sources with standalone skins taking precedence, matching runtime behavior. Copy needs a browser with clipboard access; Download and Log are alternatives. Reloading starts a new clean history and resets selection and preview controls. Leaving the editor, refreshing, or closing its tab asks for confirmation while changes are unsaved.

Unsaved authored edits also create a browser draft after a short pause and flush when leaving or hiding the page. On the next visit to the same character and rig file, the editor offers to restore a draft when its original source still matches. Restored edits remain unsaved and can be undone in one step. Declining recovery, exporting, resetting, or undoing back to the saved baseline removes the draft. Drafts are local to this browser and deployment path, with the latest draft shared between tabs for the same file. They exclude selection, camera and preview settings, and do not replace JSON exports. Invalid drafts, incompatible rig topology, and changed source files are rejected. If browser storage is disabled or full, the recovery status advises downloading JSON while normal editing remains available.

Undo history retains the most recent 100 operations by default, dropping the oldest entries as the limit is reached. Entries share unchanged frozen rig data; undo/redo and dirty checks do not serialize or parse the rig. Embedded editors can configure this with `new RigEditorState(rig, rigFile, { historyLimit: 250 })`; pass that state to `RigEditor`. The limit must be a nonnegative safe integer; zero disables undo/redo. Grouped drags and field edits count as one operation. Saved baselines and full-rig reset remain available after older history is dropped.

Field values are checked immediately while editing. Full rig validation runs when a drag or numeric field session finishes; the panel shows Pending during edits. Validate and export always check the current rig, including unfinished edits.

Releasing a drag outside the canvas, switching windows or tabs, or leaving the page ends the gesture. Authored changes commit as one undo entry and are validated; camera panning stops without changing rig history. Returning to the editor allows a fresh drag immediately.

For embedded editors, `workingCopy`, `getBone()`, `getSlot()`, and `getAttachment()` return frozen readonly data. Use the state's `editBoneFields()`, `editSlotFields()`, and `editSpriteFields()` methods to preserve validation, dirty tracking, and undo history. Retained views keep their previous values after edits; request a fresh view to read the current data, or use `structuredClone(state.workingCopy)` for an independent mutable copy.

`RigEditorScene` accepts a third options argument with `characterUrl` and optional `ikChains`. URLs resolve relative to the editor document. Preview chains default to an empty list, so other rigs can load without the demo's bone names; configured chains must be valid direct two-bone chains. The shipped editor passes the demo chains explicitly and continues to load the sample character. Scene options are copied when constructed.

The DOM panel controllers expose `dispose()` for hosts that unmount or replace editor panels. Dispose the inspector, controls, hierarchy, and session controllers before destroying the game. Disposal removes their listeners, closes an inspector edit, and prevents pending imports or clipboard results from updating detached panels. A new set of controllers can then mount on the same host elements. The shipped page performs this cleanup during hot module replacement.
